ac·modern doesn't make you compliant: your auditor tests your controls, not your tools. What it gives you is a consistent process and the records auditors ask for. Here's where it usually helps. Check the wording of your own controls with your auditor.

## SOC 2 (2017 Trust Services Criteria)

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="CC6.1: Logical access security">
    A catalog of systems with named owners and defined access levels, privileged levels marked, single sign-on with SSO-required mode, and an audit log of access changes. Password sign-ins can require [two-step verification](/identity/two-step-verification) (TOTP), and `/api/v1/users` reports who has it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CC6.2: Registering and authorising users before granting access">
    Every grant starts as a request with a justification and documented approval (approver, capacity, time) before setup. New-hire access is requested through onboarding. Removals on offboarding and expiry are recorded. Evidence: the population CSV and tickets.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CC6.3: Changing and removing access, least privilege">
    Transfers request new access and remove old access together. Permission changes are their own request type. Privileged levels need the system owner. Time-limited access expires on its own. Periodic access reviews have exportable decisions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CC7.2: Monitoring, and the integrity of the audit trail">
    Every access change and setting change is in the audit log. Each day is sealed with a chained SHA-256 digest that admins can verify at any time, the seals can be exported, and every event can be streamed to your SIEM or log store as it happens. See [Audit log integrity](/compliance/audit-log).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A

| Control | How ac·modern helps |
|---|---|
| **A.5.15** Access control | Per-system rules: owner, approval rule, levels, durations |
| **A.5.16** Identity management | Directory sync from your IdP; offboarding when the IdP deactivates someone |
| **A.5.17** Authentication information | Only partly: ac·modern delegates sign-in to your IdP with SSO, and can require two-step verification for its own password sign-ins. It doesn't manage passwords for other systems. |
| **A.5.18** Access rights | Requests, approvals, setup, expiry, removal and periodic reviews, all recorded |
| **A.8.15** Logging | Audit log of access and control changes, daily tamper-evident seals, streaming to your log store |
| **A.8.2** Privileged access rights | Privileged levels, owner approval, short durations, privileged-only evidence |

## What auditors usually ask for

- The population of access changes for the period: **Audit evidence → Population CSV**
- Tickets for samples: **Print tickets** (one page each, with the record fingerprint)
- Access reviews: **Reviews → Export evidence**
- Terminated employees' access removal: offboarding removals in the population (type *Removal*, reason *Offboarding*)
- Who can administer the tool itself: **ac·modern admin changes** on the evidence page
- A description of the process: **Control description** on the evidence page
- That the audit trail is complete and unaltered: **Audit log → Verify now**, the downloaded seals, or your streamed copy
