title: Importing apps from your IdP
description: Turn the apps your identity provider already manages into resources, with who has them today.
icon: scan-search
---

Your identity provider already knows which apps you use and who can sign in to each. **Resources → Import from IdP** reads that, so you start with a complete inventory and a recorded baseline of access instead of a hand-typed list.

It works with **Authentik**, **Okta** and **Microsoft Entra ID**, once connected under **Settings → Integrations**. The scan only reads; nothing changes in your IdP.

## What gets imported

For each app you tick:

| In your IdP | In ac·modern |
|---|---|
| The app | A resource with its name, launch URL and description |
| Each **group** that grants it (Authentik group binding, Okta group assignment, Entra group assignment) | An access level named after the group. Approved requests add people to that group. |
| People assigned **directly** | A *Direct assignment* level. In Okta it's provisioned by app assignment; in Authentik and Entra it's set up by hand. |
| Everyone who can use it today | **Recorded access** with the source *IdP*, so it's in [reviews](/guides/access-reviews), [offboarding](/guides/offboarding) and the [audit evidence](/compliance/audit-evidence) |

If every level maps to a group (or an Okta app assignment), the resource is set to be **set up by** that IdP. Otherwise it's set up by hand, and you can change that on the resource.

You become each resource's owner, and your organisation's default approval rule applies. Review both afterwards: the owner is who approves under *system owner* rules, so pick the real one.

## Before you scan

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Authentik">
    The integration's API token must be able to read **applications**, **policy bindings** and **users**, as well as change groups. A token for a superuser service account covers it.

    Authentik apps with no bindings are open to everyone with an Authentik account; they're marked *Open to everyone*. Apps gated only by expression policies (not groups or users) are imported with one level and no recorded access.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Okta">
    The token's admin needs to read apps and their assignments: **Read-only Administrator** plus the roles the Okta integration already needs, or a Super Admin. Okta's own apps (Dashboard, Admin Console, browser plugin) are left out.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Entra ID">
    Add the **application** permission `Application.Read.All` to the app registration (with admin consent), alongside `GroupMember.ReadWrite.All`. The scan lists **Enterprise applications**. If an app doesn't require assignment and nobody is assigned, it's marked *Open to everyone*.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Scanning

1. **Resources → Import from IdP.** The scan runs straight away; with more than one IdP connected, pick one at the top.
2. Each app shows its groups, how many people each grants, and how many of them **aren't in ac·modern yet**. Hover over that count to see who.
3. Apps already imported are marked **Imported** and can't be picked again. ac·modern's own sign-in app is left unticked.
4. Tick the apps to track and click **Import**.

<Tip>
  Add your people first, through [directory sync](/identity/directory-sync) or invites. Access for anyone who isn't a member yet can't be recorded, and an app isn't imported twice, so record their access afterwards under **Access → Record existing access**.
</Tip>

## In the audit evidence

The import is your starting point, like [recording existing access](/guides/recording-existing-access) by hand. Each recorded grant says *Found in Authentik by an app scan (group engineering)* and is listed under **Access outside requests** for the period you imported in. After that, new access should go through requests.
